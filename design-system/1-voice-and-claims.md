# Voice and claims

Portal sounds like Chris explaining a job on site: plain, direct and specific about the work. Customer reviews mention clear communication again and again, so the copy should be as clear as a good estimate.

## How Portal writes

- Write "we" for Portal and "you" for the customer.
- Name the job and the result. "Crumbling front steps? We replace them with new concrete."
- Use concrete nouns: steps, driveway, retaining wall, slab, tent, pour.
- Keep ad headlines to about six words and one idea.
- Use sentence case everywhere. The website's buttons are in title case today; new work uses sentence case.
- The call to action is "Free estimate" in ads, video and the small website header button, and "Get a free estimate" on large web buttons.
- Use a spaced hyphen ( - ) where a sentence needs a dash. Never use an em dash.
- Use US spelling and US units (feet, square feet, miles).
- Leave exclamation points to customers. Portal's own copy has none.

## Claims you cannot use

- Prices, price ranges, start dates or timelines.
- Financing of any kind.
- Slab lifting or drainage work. Portal does not do them.
- "4.9 stars" or "95+ reviews". Both are retired.
- Any number, result or comparison you cannot point to a source for.

## Quoting customers

- Quote reviews word for word, with first name and last initial ("Sarah K.").
- Trim only from the start or end of a review, never from the middle.
- Short quotes that work anywhere, each trimmed only at its start or end:
  - "The professionalism and quality of work were top notch!" (Raam W.)
  - "Portal was the most professional by far. Fair price, great communication, beautiful finished product." (Sarah K.)
  - "Chris really knows his stuff. He walked us through the whole process and helped us choose the right finish for our patio." (Linda M.)
  - "Consistent quality and a crew that actually cares about doing things right." (Karen P.)
